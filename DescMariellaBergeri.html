Mariella bergeri

Description

Name: Ammonite ( This specimen donated by GSI to the NLC, Neyveli in 1984 and is displayed in its museum)
Genus: Mariella (formerly Turrilites)
Species: bergeri
Formation: Karai Formation
Locality: Maruvattur
Classification: Molluscs: Cephalopoda:Ammonoidea: Ammonitida: SubOrder - Ancyloceratina, Family - Turrilitidae

Mariella is an ammonoid genus, named by Nowak (1916) from the upper Albian and Cenomanian stages
the mid Cretaceous, included in the Turrilitidae. Its type is Turrilites bergeri

Description
Mariella resembles Turrilites in general form. The shell is asymmetric, closely wound in
a long expanding trochoidal spire.
Ribs are slightly oblique and are rather feeble, each with 4 more or less equally spaced tubercles.
Turrilites differs primarily in being more strongly ribbed and in having a more oval aperture.

Features in this specimen: Helicoid coiling with strong ribs and tubercles.

Distribution
Fossils of Mariella have been found in Angola, Antarctica, Australia, Brazil, Canada (British Columbia),
Colombia (Hiló Formation), France, Germany, Iran, Japan, Kazakhstan, Madagascar, Mexico, Mozambique,
New Zealand, South Africa, Spain, Switzerland, Turkmenistan, Ukraine, the United Kingdom, and
the United States (California, Texas, Oregon).

• Part L, Mollusca 4. Lawrence, Kansas: Geological Society of America and University of Kansas Press.
