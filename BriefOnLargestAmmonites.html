Largest Ammonites

Introduction

Ammonites were marine mollusks that existed during the Mesozoic era. Some species grew to enormous sizes, with shells reaching several meters in diameter.

The world's largest ammonite

The world's largest ammonite, a Parapuzosia seppenradensis, was discovered in Seppenrade, Germany, in 1895. This fossil is about 1.8 meters (5.9 ft) in diameter and is on display at the Münster Museum of Natural History. It was found in a quarry and is estimated to have weighed over 3.5 tons.

Discovery: The fossil was found in 1895 by quarrymen near Seppenrade.
Species: It belongs to the species Parapuzosia seppenradensis.
Size: The fossil measures 1.8 meters (5.9 ft) in diameter and is estimated to weigh over 3.5 tons.
Location: It is on display at the Museum of Natural History in Münster, Germany.
